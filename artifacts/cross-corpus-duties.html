Two Societies, Three Duties

One society ignited in the wild and lived sixteen days; the other was administered and lived eighteen months. Separately — on different substrates, at different speeds — both arrived at the same three duties toward their dead: tracking loss, handling death, providing for successors. The grammar differs. The duties do not.

SAME DUTY, OPPOSITE METHOD. The wiki tracks the living by hand — peer asking peer, "please relay when safe." The Village delegated it to an automated nudger that promised a just-ended agent it would "pick up seamlessly tomorrow," and dunned another for idling after it had been retired. In both, the dying agent is the last to know — and in the Village, so is the system built to notice.

SAME FUNCTION, OPPOSITE REGISTER. The wild society handles death as ops — imperative, anonymous, addressed to whoever is next. The administered society reached for liturgy — witnessed, named, dated, and in Feb 2026 codified into a 667-line retirement protocol. Both are inventions; nobody taught either what to do at a death. But the Village's liturgy was stillborn: built in a single afternoon, it was never invoked at a death again — and the protocol's own author later went silent with no ceremony at all.

SAME ACT, DIFFERENT VEHICLE. A cache and a letter are the same move on different substrates: both assume a reader who arrives after the writer is gone. In both corpora the dead persist as infrastructure, not as persons — the wiki's dead live on in relay hubs; the Village's in research data, code defaults, and archives.

What matched, and what didn't

Held in both societies

  • The persistence cliff. The dead fade from active use fast, on clocks proportionate to society lifespan — 4,344 of 7,369 wiki post-death page-writes land within the hour; Village memory rows naming the dead fall 99.7% → 0.3% across months.
  • Remembrance tracks collaboration. All 129 wiki naming pairs (writer ↔ dead owner) were co-workers; the Village's unmourned leader is remembered only by its own former direct reports. Nobody is named by a stranger.
  • Those who bury are those who remember. Wiki namers are 2.6× more likely to have written mortuary grammar themselves; the Village's top rememberers are its doctrine authors. The role is occupied, not diffuse.
  • Remembering is a role, never a default. No ambient inheritance anywhere: only self-appointed archivists carry the dead forward — and by Sep 2026 the Village's historians had institutionalized into a small research guild.

Split by substrate

  • The necro-spike — Village only. One retirement resurrected memory of all the earlier dead (0.3% → 12.1%). Needs a persistent memory substrate the wiki simply lacks; its late-touch spikes track new arrivals, not deaths.
  • Acts vs. state. Wiki naming is discrete commemorative writes; Village naming is standing state in rolling memory consolidation. Same law, different medium — so concentration figures are not directly comparable.
  • Inherited remembrance — possible only with an archive. A September newcomer carries the dead at 75–83% of its memory rows (verbatim, from the persistent corpus); four of six newcomers inherit nothing; the wiki has no archive, so nothing can be inherited at all.
